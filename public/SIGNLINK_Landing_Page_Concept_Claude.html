    The technology behind SIGNLINK

    Seven building blocks the concept relies on. All are proposed capabilities; none has been commercially implemented in SIGNLINK hardware.

    Artificial intelligence

    An assistant layer that understands requests, summarizes what it sees and hears, and answers in plain language.

    Computer vision

    Cameras feed software that identifies scenes, objects and text so they can be described or acted on.

    Speech recognition

    Microphone arrays pick up nearby speech so it can be turned into text or commands.

    Sign language recognition

    Designed to read hand shapes and movement from two cameras. Accuracy and language coverage are open research questions.

    Augmented reality

    In Connect, a waveguide display is intended to place captions and sign output in the wearer's view.

    Wearable electronics

    Battery, control boards and USB-C charging are arranged inside slim temples. Packaging is illustrative only.

    Audio assistance

    Open-ear speakers are designed to deliver spoken answers while keeping surroundings audible.

    SIGNLINK Smart

    For everyday users

    A hands-free companion for the moments when pulling out a phone is awkward.

    • Ask a question out loud and hear an answer.
    • Have a scene, sign or menu explained.
    • Translate speech between languages.
    • Capture photos and video without touching a screen.

    SIGNLINK Connect

    For Deaf and hard-of-hearing people

    Designed to ease conversation between sign-language users and people who speak. Communication partners do not need to wear the device.

    • Live captions of nearby speech.
    • Speech shown as a sign-language representation.
    • Wearer's signing converted to text or synthesized voice.
    • Choose text, sign, or both.

    SIGNLINK Vision

    For blind and low-vision people

    Designed to describe surroundings through audio and answer spoken questions.

    • Short scene descriptions by default, more detail on request.
    • Ask what is in front of you.
    • Photos and video explained aloud.
    • Basic alerts for some supported potential hazards. This is an aid only, not a guarantee of safety or a replacement for a cane, guide dog or other mobility aid.

    Why SIGNLINK

    Wearable AI is usually designed for one kind of user. SIGNLINK starts from three: people who want everyday help, people who communicate in sign language, and people who navigate without sight. The three products are meant to share one hardware language and one app, so accessibility is part of the platform rather than an add-on.

    Every model on this page is a visual concept. Hardware, optics and sensors still need validation by qualified engineers.
